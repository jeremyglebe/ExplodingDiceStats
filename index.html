That’s really the whole trick

Three ideas worth keeping.

01

One die vs. a target

p = (1/n)⌊(t−1)/n⌋ × (1 − ((t−1) mod n)/n)

Count the required explosions, then the successful faces on the final roll.

02

Any die can succeed

p = 1 − (failure₁ × failure₂ × …)

It is easier to find the chance that every die fails, then flip it.

03

Added dice vs. a target

p = 1 − P(sum is below target)

Build each die’s exact outcomes, combine them, then count the failures.

Added exploding dice

Exact odds, no simulation

Combine the failures.
Then flip them.

For added dice, there is no single shortcut formula. Instead, the calculator builds each die’s exact outcome distribution and combines those distributions one die at a time.

  1. 01

    List each die’s possible totals

    On an exploding dn, a final total has the form k·n + r, where k is the number of explosions and r is a final roll from 1 to n−1.

    P(total = k·n + r) = 1 / nk+1
  2. 02

    Combine one die at a time

    For every old total and every possible result on the next die, multiply their probabilities and add that chance to the new combined total. This operation is called a convolution.

    P(sum = s) = Σ P(old = x) × P(next = s−x)
  3. 03

    Keep only the failures

    We only need combined totals below the target. Anything at or above it has already succeeded, so it can be left out of the next round of calculation.

  4. 04

    Subtract from 100%

    Add all remaining failure probabilities, then flip the answer.

    success = 1 − P(combined total < target)
